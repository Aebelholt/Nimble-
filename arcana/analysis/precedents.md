# Precedents: 4e and Draw Steel

How two other games handle items stronger than the baseline, checked against the copies you uploaded (4e DMG pp. 163-165; Draw Steel Heroes, Rewards chapter, pp. 313, 326-337). The source files are not committed to the repo.

## 4e artifacts (DMG p. 164)

- **Outside the economy.** Artifacts "have a level but no price"; they can't be bought, crafted or destroyed by normal means, and the DM decides who gets one.
- **Concordance.** A score from 0 to 20, starting at 5. One to three positive and one to three negative modifiers, each a deed tied to the artifact's goals; +1d10 when the wielder gains a level. Attitude bands: Pleased 16-20, Satisfied 12-15, Normal 5-11, Unsatisfied 1-4, Angered 0 or lower.
- **Powers by attitude.** Base powers always work. Greater powers unlock only at a high enough attitude, and "rarely more than five levels above the character's level". Bad attitude weakens powers or adds penalties.
- **The score stays hidden.** The wielder knows what raises or lowers it, never how close the next change is.
- **Moving on.** Every artifact leaves, after one to three levels as a rule of thumb. The DM plans its exit before handing it over. A pleased artifact leaves quietly at a level-up; an angered one leaves at the end of the encounter.
- **Its own use budget.** Artifact daily powers don't count against the limit on magic item daily powers.

## Draw Steel treasures (Rewards chapter)

- **Four kinds** (p. 313): consumables (one use), trinkets (permanent, never fade), leveled treasures (grow with the hero), artifacts ("can change or shape an adventure, or even an entire campaign").
- **Leveled treasures** (p. 326): benefits at 1st, 5th and 9th level, cumulative. "Carry three safely": with more than three, a Presence test each respite, and on a bad result the items make you drop the others.
- **Each treasure lists** an item prerequisite, a project source and a project goal for crafting. The prerequisite is a world object ("a lash used to punish a mutineer"), which the Director can turn into a side quest.
- **Artifacts** (p. 336): "Make no mistake, these items unbalance the game ... That's part of the fun!" They are sentient and want to move on, and armies, dragons and gods come for a wielder who won't let go. The Blade of a Thousand Years shows the shape: huge powers, a pact with a deadline, and a price if it fails.
- **Not in this file:** Malice, the Director's resource. It is referenced here but defined in *Draw Steel: Monsters*.

## What this means for Sejr

| Precedent | Sejr already has | Gap |
|-----------|------------------|-----|
| Artifacts are labelled game-breaking | Majors in the draw, by your choice | The cards don't say so. A "campaign-shaping" tag would answer both audits. |
| Concordance from listed deeds | Unlock deeds, escalation | Unlock gates judged on sincerity ("genuine", "the bell listens"). Concordance swaps the judgment for listed deeds and keeps the mood. |
| Every artifact moves on, exit planned in advance | Finite relics; Seven-Leaf buds; Sown Teeth runs out | Most Majors never leave. A planned exit caps a campaign-shaper without nerfing it. |
| Carry three safely | No arcana limit | Nothing stops one hero collecting several faction rewards. |
| Leveled benefits at 1/5/9 | Sown Teeth die steps at 5 and 9 | Other cards don't scale, so a level 1 Major stays the same strength at level 10. |
| Item prerequisite as a world object | Unlocks and refills are world actions | Already the deck's strongest habit. |
