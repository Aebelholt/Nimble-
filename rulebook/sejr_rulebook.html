Sejr
a drop-in Nimble campaign in the City of Arches

The city is thousands of years old and nobody controls its arches. Keys exist, and whoever holds one decides where an arch opens. Patrons hold plenty, so the city's politics are route politics: who can move people and goods wins. Out past the walls, the places nobody travels to any more are quietly going wrong.

You are professionals with lives of your own, thrown together on gigs. Each of you carries an arcanum you haven't figured out yet, and a place in the city to come home to. One gig a session, played by whoever shows up.

How we play

the table, the system, and what's different here

The table

  • One gig a session. Three hours, whoever shows. Every session is a bottle episode that works for one player or a full table.
  • The arch is the door. The map is drawn on the mat, and the arch is the way in and out of it.
  • Absent heroes are busy elsewhere in the city. Their players tick an arcana ritual instead of losing ground.
  • Levels follow the group. New and returning players join at the average group level, as the same hero with a beard.
  • Milestones. The group levels every two or three episodes, so attendance never splits the party.
  • Renown rises by 1 each episode and is capped at the average group level. It sets the Stage and how hard the rooms get.
  • Fail forward. A failure moves a clock. It never ends the session.

Nimble, for 5e players

Sejr runs on Nimble 2. If you know 5e you know most of it: the six familiar abilities fold into STR, DEX, INT and WIL, attacks always roll damage (a 1 on the Primary Die misses, the highest number crits and explodes), and on your turn you have three actions. Monsters don't: they move, use what's on their stat block, and their turn ends.

Anything this book doesn't say works as the Nimble Core Rules say.

Gifts and traits

Every hero has two gifts, one word for each of their class's two key stats (Quick, Studied, Steady), and two traits (Proud, Loyal, Restless).

  • Invoke a gift or trait in a dramatic moment for advantage. Once per hero per episode, at the GM's call.
  • Suffer a trait: take disadvantage because of who you are, and tick your arcana ritual.

Skills and specialties

The A5e skill list is the table's vocabulary (see Make a hero). When you roll, argue which Nimble skill applies from your sheet and the fiction. A stretch gets disadvantage, never a flat no.

A specialty gives advantage. You start with two, plus your INT if it's positive, and gain one more at levels 5, 9, 13 and 17.

Shared history. The first time two heroes share a gig, each may rewrite one life event to include the other and gain a specialty. At most two per hero, each with a different partner.

Coming and going

The session opens with a last time line: one sentence per hero, so anyone who missed a week walks straight back in.

Make a hero

fifteen minutes, then out the door

Make a hero

  1. Class. Pick one, or roll d12 and reroll a 12. Note its two key stats.
  2. Ancestry. Pick any from the Nimble books.
  3. Stats. Pick an array and put the highest numbers in your key stats.
  4. Gifts and traits. One gift word for each key stat; two traits.
  5. Three life events. Origin, Youth, Recent. Answer the questions, pick one skill from each, and keep any tie you like as a thread.
  6. Specialties. Two, plus INT if positive, in skills you picked.
  7. Arcanum. Draw one, unexamined. Don't read the card yet.
  8. Holding or guild. A place in the city, or your class guild.
  9. Look, habit, mark, secret. Roll or pick.
  10. Level 1, or the average group level. Then roll the first gig.

Arcana

the thing you carry and haven't worked out yet

What an arcanum is

Every hero starts with an arcanum: an object with a story and a power nobody has worked out yet. It starts unexamined. The card has six parts.

PartWhat it does
ObjectWhat it looks like and where it came from. You know this much.
UnlockA ritual of two or three deeds, ticked off in play. When the last box fills, the arcanum wakes.
RiskWhat can go wrong on the final deed.
ResourceOne tracker the powers spend or fill: pips, a d6 against accumulation, a toggle, or Wounds.
PowersWhat it does once awake. At least one spends the resource; at least one is free or passive.
EscalationWhat happens when the resource runs dry or overflows. Usually someone comes for it.

The arcana ritual

Each hero has one ritual track: the unlock boxes on their card. A box ticks when you do the deed in play. It also ticks once when you are absent, and once when you suffer a trait.

The final box is only ever filled at the table.

University ranks follow the ritual: Novice (unexamined), Reader (first trait revealed), Wielder (reliable use; patron jobs open), Cracker (broken open). Enrolling is optional. A student gets the job board, a trade chapter (a specialty and a holding upgrade) and arcana training between episodes. Without enrolling, an arcanum advances only through play.

Table rules for arcana

Off-screen refills

  • Between episodes, each hero may make one refill off-screen, if it can be done inside the city. An off-screen refill restores 1 pip, or clears 1 point of accumulation, never the whole tracker: a vigil, a game of chance, a retelling to a new listener, a burial on the Cliffs of the Dead, hair, blood or cloth freely given by someone outside the party (Hexing Needles). Absent players may use it too, alongside their arcana-ritual tick.
  • Say it in one sentence in the next episode's "last time" line.
  • A refill that needs a place beyond the city needs a gig: the Revlend Marshes, Bloodrust Depths, the Lost Cavern of the Sundering Saurians, a world beyond an arch. That gig is a ready-made hero stake, and it takes the party to a region your Neglect clocks track.

Readings

  • Cards that read lies, motives, names or history give true answers. A true answer can still point at the wrong person: the liar was covering for someone, the owner sold it twice, the fear is about something else. Use it when two readers at one table would solve the gig in its first scene.

Unknowns are rolls

  • The campaign uses the City of Arches as a base and does its own thing. Nothing is canon until it hits the table.
  • Cards marked [TABLE ROLL] in their notes lean on a fact the books don't settle (will the Observatory keep a lens overnight? does Vathrex sell coal?). Don't decide it in prep. The hero asks, and rolls Influence against the GM's sense of how likely it is. On a failure they find another way: sneak the lens in, steal the coal, pay someone who can.

Escalations and the thread board

  • An Escalation that brings a new threat writes a thread card only if the board has a free slot. If the board is full, it ticks the closest existing clock instead, and the threat arrives through that thread.

Long durations

  • A week, a lunar cycle, a full moon or a month on a card passes off-screen between episodes. Say it in the "last time" line. Absent players count it the same way.

Guilds

the alternative to a holding

Holding or guild

At hero creation, take a holding or join your class's guild.

A guild gives a shared hall to Safe Rest in, one contact in the guild, one specialty from the guild's skill, and the guild as a door that sends you jobs.

It costs the guild's ask, every arc. Skip it for a whole arc and drop a rank; an Apprentice who skips it is out.

  • Apprentice: you join here.
  • Journeyman: meet the ask once and finish one gig for the guild; receive your class arcanum or the lead to it.
  • Master: take a side in the guild's split and win the vote; you hold the guild's seat.

A guild gives its arcanum only to its own class. Another class gets the contact, the specialty and the jobs.

The episode

how a session is rolled and run

The session loop

seven steps, three doors
  1. Last time. One sentence per hero. Absent players tick their arcana ritual and may make one off-screen refill.
  2. Pick a door. City (trouble at home), Arch (a patron with a key) or a Thread card from the board. If nobody picks, the hottest clock goes first. Or roll d6: 1–3 City, 4–6 Arch.
  3. Where. City: roll the Stage. Arch: roll patron, debt and far side.
  4. The job. Roll the hook and the But.
  5. The map. Three to five areas, each rolled on Rooms.
  6. Play. At every scene change, roll But+.
  7. Close. One thread closes and one opens. Renown +1 (capped). Absent players tick. Neglect advances for skipped places. Level up every two or three episodes.

Running a patron

  • Reactions aren't rolled. When the party refuses or betrays a patron, it answers with its Method and its currency.
  • Stages move on table choices only. A patron's stage is a three-step track; But+ can foreshadow a step, never take it.
  • Secrets. Each patron has one. Fix it now, or write three candidates and lock one in at first contact.
  • A party-owned key is a mid-campaign prize. It offends one patron and becomes a thread.

Bia

the GM's hero, kept to the edges

Bia, the minotaur paladin

Bia anchors the party when attendance is thin. She must never become the spotlight: she never knows the answer, never solves the scene, and counts as a hero for the encounter budget so a lone player still gets a fair fight.

Frenzy

When Bloodied, Bia saves. On a fail she becomes the labyrinth beast and attacks the nearest creature. A player runs her in frenzy, never the GM.

Turning back

Pick two or three: she is healed above Bloodied; someone speaks her name or oath words (Influence DC 12); three rounds pass; she harms someone she loves.

The tally

Mark paladin each time she turns back by choice, beast each time she needs rescue. When one side reaches its number, the table learns who she is. At level 3 the tally can pick her subclass.

  1. Object. d30, or d4: on a 1 add a material, on a 2 make it an amulet instead.
  2. Nature. A kind of magic and an element.
  3. Lore lives in one source (d8). Examining it rolls d6 on the clue table.
  4. Ritual. Two different unlock deeds (d7) and a risk (d5).
  5. Resource. A name (d10), a size (d4: 3, 3, 4, 5) and a refill (d7).
  6. Power (d8), a quirk that's always on (d10), and an escalation (d13).
  7. Name it in play, and check it against the deck's design rules before it reaches the table.

Escalation

1–13Escalation
1The object chooses a champion. It starts to change your body to keep itself safe; the GM names one change each time it empties.
2Something has been following the object for a long time. When it empties, it arrives, and it can look like anyone.
3Your fingertips blacken. Each time it empties, the black climbs higher, and it grants one boon and one oddity.
4It stores your pain. When it empties, take every Wound you avoided since you last emptied it.
5You stop aging. When it empties, a year catches up with you all at once.
6It goes silent for a season. What it knows is locked away until a ritual wakes it.
7It shatters, and the shards go into your flesh. You keep the power, and it keeps you.
8You are branded. Those who carry arcana of their own can see the mark.
9You can't bring yourself to put it down. If it is taken, you will hunt for it.
10It must be fed each week. Miss a week, and it feeds on whoever is closest.
11It gets heavier after every fight, until you shed what it has caught.
12Water undoes it. A soaking costs it 1 RES, and it can't be used while wet.
13An order that hates its kind attacks you on sight.
