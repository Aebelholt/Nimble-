Start Here

Nimble is a fast-paced, heroic fantasy TTRPG where
bold choices and epic stories take center stage.
At least one player should know the rules in this
book (no need for everyone to read it). The Game
Master’s Guide teaches the rules as you play.
If you’re new to TTRPGs, use a premade hero
(see the box below). Every hero class is easy to pick
up at level 1, giving a taste of the fantasy and introducing the rules gradually. Complexity and tactical
depth build as you level up.
If you’re experienced with TTRPGs, feel free
to create your own hero from scratch (see Character Creation on pg. 18 for more). Start with your

character sheet as you go.

The Golden Rule

Use Common Sense, Be Cool. Nimble’s rules are written to read naturally and stay compact, not to cover
every case. If something doesn’t make sense, it doesn’t work (e.g., Grappling from across the room or with
your hands full, Blinding an eyeless Ooze, tripping an 80 ft. tall Dragon, Restraining a Fire Elemental with
flammable rope, Poisoning a Skeleton, a cheesy tactic that feels like it shouldn’t work). When in doubt, the
GM gets the final say.

How to Be a Good Player

Be heroic. Create a hero eager to dive into the
shared adventure with the party. Reluctant, anti-social loners can drag down everyone’s fun.
Pay attention. Ask questions. Take notes.
The more questions you ask, the more you’ll have
to work with, and you’ll be able to make more interesting decisions.
Be creative! Frequently, the answer to a problem isn’t found on your character sheet. A hammer
(or anything else) can be used in lots of ways!
Embrace failure. You shouldn’t try to fail, but
some of the best moments can come from a failed
roll or a bad decision. Go with it!
Respect everyone else’s time. Arrive on time.
While playing, swift, suboptimal decisions are better
than slow, optimal ones. They will tend to be more
fun anyway! If you can’t decide what to do on your
turn, attack and pass. Think when everyone else isn’t
waiting on you.
Share the spotlight. If you’ve been rolling or
talking more than everyone else, call on your party-

mates to help you. Sneaky heroes shouldn’t scout a
whole dungeon alone. A charismatic hero can lead
a negotiation (that’s fine!) and still call on his Berserker pal for a bit of extra leverage. “Ragnar, would
you like to have a word with this stubborn gentleman
while the rest of us step outside?”
Treat the world realistically. If you insult (or
kill) other characters you come across, expect consequences! If you negotiate and haggle with every
merchant, your reputation as penny pinchers will
likely become widespread. Threaten a king, expect
to be jailed (or worse). If you heal an ally, deal lots of
damage, or always use the same tactics, expect the
bad guys to catch on and adapt.
Be gracious to the GM. They put a lot into running the game; avoid arguing over rulings. Give
them a break sometimes and offer to run a session
for them! Bring snacks.

CORE RULES
Stats

Heroes have 4 stats that impact their effectiveness in various tasks: 2 Key Stats crucial to their class and 2
Secondary Stats. The maximum a hero’s stat can typically go is +5.

Strength (STR)

Your raw physical power and resilience, endurance, and resistance to harm. Affects
STR weapon damage, resistance to Wounds, HP recovery, Concentration, STR
saves, carrying capacity, Grappling, and the Might skill.

Dexterity (DEX)

Your agility, reflexes, and precision with blades or bows. Affects DEX weapon
damage, Initiative, DEX saves, Grappling, and can contribute to Defense, as well
as the Stealth and Finesse skills.

Intelligence (INT)

Your Intelligence reflects knowledge and reasoning across fields like the arcane,
tactics, or street smarts. Affects languages, spellcasting, use of wands, spell scrolls,
INT saves, as well as the Arcana, Examination, and Lore skills.

Will (WIL)

Your force of personality, courage, and wisdom. Will shapes your interactions
with both nature and society. Affects spellcasting and WIL saves, as well as the
Insight, Influence, Naturecraft, and Perception skills.

KEY. When an ability or spell references “KEY,” use one of your Key Stats. If a stat is listed before a die roll, roll a number of
dice equal to the stat. For example, “WIL d8” means that if your WIL is 2, you roll 2d8 (two eight-sided dice).

Skill Checks & Saves

When you want to affect the world (convince an NPC,
spot a trap, pick a lock, etc.), the GM may call for a
skill check. Roll 1d20 and add your skill. If the total
meets or exceeds the Difficulty Challenge (DC), you
succeed; otherwise, you fail. The DC varies based
on the task (a roll of 1 always fails; a roll of 20
always succeeds):
• Easy: Spotting a large Ogre crouched behind a
small bush might be a DC 8 Perception check.
• Medium: A hidden doorway behind a bookcase
might be a DC 12 Examination check.
• Challenging: Calming an injured Owlbear stuck
in a trap may be a DC 15 Naturecraft check.
• Very Difficult: Intuiting the true intentions of a
trained Spy may be a DC 18 Insight check.
• Extremely Difficult: Disarming an ancient legendary trap may be a DC 20 Finesse check.

Saves

When the world affects you, make a save instead of
a skill check. Roll 1d20 and add the relevant stat. A
roll of 1 always fails, 20 always saves.
• STR Save. When your overall fitness and physicality are tested. STR helps resist forced movement,
restraint, poison, and extreme temperatures.
• DEX Save. When your agility or speed is tested.
DEX helps you dive for cover in an explosion or
stay on your feet while running across icy terrain.
• INT Save. When your intelligence is tested. INT
helps you see through tricks and illusions.
• WIL Save. When your courage or personality is
tested. WIL helps you resist charm or fear effects.

Heroes & Saves

• The DC for effects a hero causes is 10+KEY.
• Each hero has 1 advantaged save (▲), 1 disadvantaged save (▼), and 2 neutral saves.
Example. A Berserker (STR▲, INT▼) would roll all
of his STR saves with advantage and all of his INT saves
with disadvantage (see next page).

Concentration

Some activities require Concentration to maintain.
A character can only Concentrate on one activity at a
time. Whenever a character is crit while concentrating, they must make a DC 10 STR save or Concentration is broken and the activity fails. Concentration
automatically ends (and cannot be begun) when a
character is at 0 HP or is Incapacitated.

Cover

A creature mostly obscured from line of sight (standing behind a tree, a larger ally, a knocked over table,
in poor lighting, etc.) has Cover and imposes disadvantage on attacks against them. A creature completely obscured from view has Full Cover and
cannot typically be targeted by attacks.

Hiding

To hide in combat, you must have Cover from the
creatures you are attempting to hide from and use
an action to make a DC 10 Stealth check (if you
have Full Cover, you succeed automatically).
Most creatures will not prioritize finding or attacking hidden creatures (as long as there are other
dangers to deal with). The first
attack you make while
hidden has advantage (then you are
no longer hidden).
Monsters Are
Smart! They
may catch on to
heroes using the
same tactics over
and over again!
Hiding isn’t magic.
If you’re hiding behind
a tree, and a monster
looks behind the tree,
you will be seen!

Advantage & Disadvantage

If you’re ever in a favorable situation, the GM may allow you to roll with advantage: roll 1 additional die
of the same type and remove the lowest. Alternatively, if the situation is unfavorable or your idea is a long
shot, you may have to roll with disadvantage (removing the highest die instead). For multiple instances of
advantage/disadvantage: roll 1 extra die for each and remove the lowest/highest.

Example 1. A short sword (1d6) with
advantage. Roll 1 additional die and
remove the lowest: You hit!

Example 2. A greataxe (2d6) with disadvantage 2.
Roll 2 additional dice, remove the 2 highest. You
miss (see Attack on pg. 14 for more)!

Note. If there is a tie,
always remove dice from
left to right. 1 advantage
cancels out 1 disadvantage before you roll.

Hit Points & Dying

Hit Points (HP) represent your ability to endure
damage. Damage reduces your HP; when reduced
to 0 HP, gain 1 Wound. You’re also Dying until you
regain HP. While Dying, your actions are limited to
1, Concentration ends, and you are at risk of further
serious harm:
• Attacking causes 1 Wound unless you succeed on
a DC 10 STR save.
• Taking damage while Dying causes 2 Wounds; a
crit causes 3 instead.

Temporary HP

Some abilities or effects may grant temporary HP
(temp HP); these are reduced first when taking
damage. Temp HP do not combine: If a hero has
temp HP and would gain more, they instead choose
which amount to keep. They expire after a Safe Rest.

Hit Dice

Hit Dice (HD) represent your ability to recover from
minor injuries. Heroes start with a max of 1 Hit Die
at level 1, and this limit increases by 1 each time

they level up. Hit Dice are spent to regain HP while
Resting and are recovered during a Safe Rest (see
Safe Rests on pg. 17 for more).

Wounds

Wounds are serious injuries you’ve taken; they are
a long-term gauge of how close you are to death.
HP can usually be recovered quickly, but Wounds
may take many days of Resting to fully recover from
(usually 1/Safe Rest).

Death

You die when you have taken 6 Wounds (unless an
ability changes this). There are ways to revive a hero
who has died, but they are rare and often come at
a very steep cost.
Gritty Dying Rules. For a more challenging or “old school” game reduce the max
number of Wounds heroes have: 5 Wounds
for slightly harder, all the way down to 2
Wounds for a very lethal game!

Movement & Size

Play usually uses a grid of 1-inch spaces (squares or hexes), each about 5 ft. (1.5m). Speed is how far you
move with one action (usually 6 spaces). You can move through allies, and through enemies at least 2 sizes
bigger or smaller than you, but you can’t stop in an occupied space.
Alternate Options. Want crunchier or looser movement? See Optional Variant Rules on pg. 54.

Creature Sizes

• Tiny. Fits in a pocket (many per space).
• Small. Fits in a backpack (2 per space).
• Medium. Human sized (1 per space).
• Large. Bear sized (2×2 area).
• Huge. Small house sized (3×3 area).
• Gargantuan. Fortress or bigger (4×4+ area).

Grappling, Riding, & Restrained

• Grappling. Use an action to make a DC 10 Might
or Finesse check on a creature your size or smaller
to Grapple it: Attacks against either of you have
advantage. If it’s smaller, it can’t move, but you can
drag it along when you move; if you’re the same
size, either of you can move at half Speed together.
• Riding. Grab a bigger creature and you’re Riding
it instead: you move with it, it attacks you with
disadvantage, and attacks that miss you strike it.
• Restrained. Held by something rather than someone (sticky tar, chains, rope, roots): you can’t move,
and attacks against you have advantage.
Breaking Free. The grabber can let go for free on their
turn. Otherwise escaping takes an action and a STR or
DEX save, or any other sensible means: teleporting,
being forced apart (e.g., Knockback), death, Incapacitation, picking a lock, cutting/burning vines, etc. A crit
on either creature also ends a grapple.

Reach, Range, & AoEs

• Reach X. Affects creatures within X spaces, and
works normally with enemies beside you. Any item
or ability without a Reach or Range has Reach 1.
• Range X. Affects creatures up to X spaces away.
Roll with disadvantage while any enemy is next to
you. You can take disadvantage for +2 Range (max
+6 additional Range; disadvantage 3).
• AoE. Area of Effect (AoE) abilities do not miss or crit,
and a single roll is applied to all targets in the area.
Ignore (dis)advantage from conditions on individual targets (e.g., Blind, Grappling), but anything
granting you (dis)advantage still applies as normal.

Some attacks, traps, spells, or other effects can also inflict conditions—usually negative effects other than
damage. Some conditions are temporary, lasting as little as a single round; others may last until cured in
some way, and some can be ended by using an action to make an appropriate save.
• Blind. Can’t see. Attacks against you have advantage,
and your attacks have disadvantage.
• Dazed. Heroes lose 1 action; monsters lose their
move OR 1 attack on their next turn.
• Dying. At 0 HP. Max 1 action. Taking damage while
Dying causes 2 Wounds; a crit causes 3 instead.
• Empowered/Weakened X. Attack damage
increased/reduced by X.
• Frightened. Disadvantage on rolls when source of
fear is near; Speed halved when moving closer to it.
• Incapacitated. Can’t do anything. Attacks against
you have advantage, and melee attacks that hit, crit.
• Invisible. Cannot be seen. Your attacks have advantage, and attacks against you have disadvantage.
• Poisoned. Disadvantage (typically, healing ends).
• Prone. Movement costs twice as much, and disadvantage on attacks. Melee attacks against you have
advantage; Ranged have disadvantage. Spend 3
spaces of your Speed to stand up.

• Restrained. Can’t move. Attacks against you have
advantage.
• Silenced. Cannot cast spells or use other abilities
that require speaking (e.g., Commander’s Orders).
• Slowed. Speed halved during your next turn.
• Taunted. Disadvantage on attacks except against
the most recent taunter.
Other Minor/Rare Statuses. Minor statuses (Smoldering, Distracted, etc.) do nothing on their own but
some spells and abilities have additional effects against
such targets. For rarer conditions, see Glossary on pg.
56 for more.

Falling & Knockback

• Falling. Creatures take 1d6 bludgeoning damage
for every 2 spaces (10 ft./3m) fallen.
• Knockback. A creature knocked into something
solid takes 5 bludgeoning damage for each space
it was cut short. Huge or Gargantuan creatures
ignore forced movement and Knockback; Large
creatures are moved half as far.
Hitting Another Creature. Other creatures generally
count as solid, but the GM can resolve it however makes
sense—both may be knocked back, share the damage,
etc. (see The Golden Rule on pg. 5 for more).

Stacking Conditions? A creature either has a condition or not (e.g., Dazed/Poisoned/Slowed). Gaining these
conditions when you already have them has no additional effect (e.g., a Dazed creature that gets Dazed again does
not lose an additional action, being Poisoned again does not impose additional disadvantage, being Slowed again
does not further reduce Speed).

COMBAT
Starting Combat

A combat encounter begins when the GM tells the party to “Roll Initiative!” Each hero rolls 1d20 and adds
their Initiative bonus (typically their DEX). A single digit means they start combat with 1 action. 2 digits, they
start combat with 2. A total of 20+ (or a natural 20) means they get all 3 actions! Regardless of what a hero
rolls on Initiative, at the end of their turn they gain all 3 actions back.
Surprise. Occasionally a party may catch the bad
guys completely off guard. So the GM may grant the
party advantage on Initiative, or in extreme cases,
they may not need to roll Initiative at all—the GM
can allow them to start with all 3 actions!
Note. Merely being hidden or punching first is NOT
sufficient to gain Surprise! If an enemy is on guard or
at all aware of your presence, they cannot be surprised.

If the heroes are surprised, they may have to roll with
disadvantage. Or, in extreme cases, the heroes may
automatically start combat with only 1 action, or the
monsters may go first also!

Turns, Rounds, & Encounters

A Turn is when a hero or group of monsters acts. A
Round is when all creatures have taken a turn (creatures act simultaneously “in-world” so Rounds and
Turns both take roughly 6 seconds). An Encounter
is all of the rounds in a particular combat.
1/Turn Abilities. If you perform one on your turn, and
can find a way to perform it on another creature’s turn,
you can do it again (e.g., Sneak Attack combined with
Opportunity Attack or Coordinated Strike!).
1/Round Abilities. These reset whenever your own
turn ends (e.g., when you Defend, you cannot do so
again until your next turn ends).

When combat begins, by default, the heroes go first.
Whichever player is ready first goes first (or whoever
makes the most sense story-wise), with play proceeding around the table clockwise.
Monsters will typically act last, though some monsters may be fast enough to act sooner in the round.
A monster (or monster group) should act at the same
time each round.

For activities that cost more than 1 action, your
actions can be spent over multiple turns in combat
as long as you keep Concentration and take no other
actions or reactions in between (unless they are free).

Encourage teamwork, be flexible. If players want
to strategically switch their turns for one round to facilitate teamwork or execute a tactical plan, the GM can
allow this at his/her discretion. This should be done only
occasionally, though, to avoid slowing the game down
or losing track of who has taken their turn.

Example. Book, the Mage, wants to cast Glacier Strike
(3 actions), but only has 1 action left. He’ll spend 1
action on this turn to start casting the spell, and on his
next turn spend 2 more to finish casting it. He’s taking
a risk, though: he can’t Defend himself and may need
to rely on his party to protect him!

Heroic Actions

Heroes have 3 actions to attack, move around the battlefield, cast spells, etc. on their turn. Generally doing
any single thing in combat costs 1 action. Some very strong spells or abilities may take more time and thus
cost more actions. All 3 actions recharge at the END of your turn, so there’s no need to save them up. Spend
them all on your turn and when you’re done, you get them all back!

Attack

Any ability (or spell) that can harm an
enemy is an attack. Roll the die on the
attack, and deal that much damage.

•Missing. If you roll a 1, the attack misses and has
no effect. For attacks with multiple dice, the leftmost die (the Primary Die) determines whether
the attack hits.
•Exploding Critical Hits. Rolling the max on a Primary Die is a critical hit (crit). When you crit, roll the
Primary Die again, and add it to the total. Repeat
this every time you roll the max—there’s no limit,
except your luck!
•Rushed Attacks. Heroes can attack more than
once on their turn, but these additional attacks are
rushed: gaining cumulative disadvantage for each
attack after the first.
Saves. For abilities that enemies must save against, they
roll with increasing advantage instead.

Cast Spell

Casting a spell requires 1 free hand (or
a held spellcasting focus), the ability
to speak, and may require mana as well. A spell’s
mana cost is equal to its spell Tier; cantrips cost
no mana. Upcasting Spells. Some spells have a
greater effect for each additional mana spent (see
Magic on pg. 44 for more).
Free Actions. These do not cost an action or any other
resource (e.g., mana) unless otherwise stated. Heroes
can do simple tasks (open an unlocked door, shout a
simple phrase, drop an item, end Concentration, etc.)
for free 1/turn.

14

Move

Traverse spaces up to your Speed this
turn (this can be broken up with other
actions). You can use multiple actions to move multiple times in one turn.
While in Difficult Terrain (e.g., water, mud,
thick roots, climbing a cliffside), each space costs
twice as much movement.

Assess

A great way to include creativity and
role-playing in combat. Choose one of
the following and then make a DC 10 skill check to:

•Ask a Question. About a weakness, ability, or
immediate plans of enemies, the environment,
story, etc. The GM will answer honestly.
•Create an Opening. Change the next Primary
Die rolled against a target this round 1 face higher
(yes, this means that attack cannot miss and is
more likely to crit as well).
•Anticipate Danger. Change ALL Primary Dice
rolled against you 1 face lower this round (yes,
this means that you cannot be crit, and attacks
miss you on 1 or 2).
Which Skill To Use? The skill you use must make sense
in the situation. For example, if you’re close to an enemy,
you could use Might to create an opening, but against
an enemy that is far away, that may not make sense. Or
if you want to ask a question about the weaknesses of
an elemental, you’d likely use Arcana.
Monsters Are Smart! You can’t
Assess using the same skill more
than once in a single encounter, as
your foes adapt to your tactics. You’ll
need to stay creative!

Heroic Reactions

Reactions cost 1 action and are generally performed when it is not your turn. A hero can perform each reaction 1/round, and they will start their turn with fewer actions. The reactions are:

Defend

Reduce incoming damage from a single
attack by your Defense. Crits ignore
Defense (other effects, like psychic damage or some
areas of effect, may likewise ignore Defense).

Interpose

Opportunity Attack

A melee attack with disadvantage
against an adjacent creature as it willingly moves away. Only heroes can make Opportunity Attacks; monsters do NOT.

Help

If a creature within Reach 2 would be hit
with an attack, you can enter their space
and become the new target of the attack
(they move to an adjacent space of their choice).

Allow an ally to reroll if you can reasonably explain how you could help
them. The GM may require a skill check (or even disallow it) if the idea is a long shot (limit: 1 Help/roll).

Interpose AND Defend? Yes! As long as you have
enough actions to spend, you can Interpose and Defend
at the same time. However, you can’t do either reaction
again until your next turn ends (each is 1/round).

Teamwork! Help as a Heroic Reaction is a fantastic
way to bring teamwork, role-playing, and creativity into
a combat encounter. Did your buddy fail? Maybe not!
Think quickly and see if you can help them succeed.

Monsters
Most monsters are unarmored, but some tougher foes might have
Medium Armor: –10 damage from incoming attacks; or Heavy Armor: –20.
Crits Ignore Armor! This means against armored foes
your tactics may need to change. Gaining advantage,
Assessing, changing weapons, or abilities can be
extremely valuable!

Vulnerability & Resistance

Some monsters may also be Vulnerable (+10 damage) or
Resistant (–10 damage) to certain kinds of attacks.

Minions

Minions are weak enemies that will die from any amount of damage.
Their feeble attacks cannot crit, ignore advantage/disadvantage, and they
attack as a group so any number of minions can be Defended against as if they
were a single attack. Dealing a large amount of damage to a minion can (at the
GM’s discretion) overflow to other minions within range as well.

Field Rests

Safe Rests

While out adventuring, heroes can take Field Rests
to regain HP.
•Catch Breath. Requires at least 10 minutes to
tend to your injuries. Expend any number of Hit
Dice one at a time (roll them and add your STR to
each), and regain that many HP.
•Make Camp. If you Rest for at least 8 hours with
food and sleep, take the max value for each Hit Die
you expend instead of rolling. Add your STR to each
Hit Die as usual.

Safe Rests take place in a safe location designated
by your GM, typically lodging at an inn overnight—
but could also be at a secret oasis, a well-stocked
cabin in the woods, near a sacred shrine, or the like.
Camping in the open wilderness or in a dungeon is
not sufficient for gaining the benefits of a Safe Rest.
After a Safe Rest, heroes recover all of their HP, Hit
Dice, mana (and other class-specific resources), and
heal 1 Wound. Safe Rests are a great opportunity for
downtime activities as well.

What if I Have Negative STR? Your frail body doesn’t
recover as quickly as others. A negative STR lowers each
Hit Die you expend (e.g., a roll of 5 with −1 STR heals 4).

Rest for HOW Long?! If your table prefers to largely
skip downtime activities and narrate past a week of Resting, that’s okay too! For a more realistic convalescence
time, 1 week/Wound recovered may make more sense.

Leveling Up

The GM may allow you to gain a level (LVL) while Safe
Resting after you complete an appropriately challenging quest or adventure. At level 1, this may be
as simple as clearing some angry rats out of a basement or rescuing someone from the woods. Each
subsequent level will generally take more effort (and
therefore more time). When you gain a level:
•HP Increase. Roll your Hit Die with advantage and
increase your max HP by that much (or you may take
the average instead: Hit Die size: d4: +3 HP; d6: +4
HP; d8: +5 HP; d10: +7 HP; d12: +8 HP).
•More Endurance. Your Hit Die max increases by 1
(it is typically equal to your level).
•More Skilled. Gain 1 skill point. Additionally, to
reflect your hero’s evolving expertise and priorities
you may move 1 point from one skill to another (as
long as the skill isn’t already negative).
•Class Features. Gain new class features for your
level. This may mean increasing your mana pool,
new spells, or even selecting a subclass!
•Other Adjustments. If any of your base stats
increase, remember to adjust other elements of your
character sheet as needed: skills change with their
respective stats, as do damage, Initiative, Defense,
mana, languages, etc.

Weapon Properties

• 2-handed. Can be held in a single hand, but must be wielded in 2 hands to attack with it.
• Load. Some weapons require extra actions to load before each shot.
• Reach. How close an enemy must be to be affected by this attack. If unspecified, Reach 1.
• Range. Attacks can be made from afar. If any enemy is adjacent to you, your Ranged attacks have disadvantage.
Add 1 die of disadvantage to gain +2 Range (max +6 additional Range; disadvantage 3).
• Thrown. Treat a melee weapon as if it had Range. Once thrown, you no longer have it!
• Vicious. Roll 1 additional die whenever you roll crit damage.
Remember! Only the Primary Die (the leftmost one) determines whether an attack crits.
Every time you roll crit damage, roll the additional Vicious die as well!

Dual Wielding

Heroes may wield 2 Light weapons at the same time. While dual
wielding, (1/round) you may gain advantage on an attack with those
weapons. You may dual wield 1-handed weapons without the Light
property if your STR is 3 or greater, or 1 weapon without the Light
property if your STR is 2. If completely unarmed, fists/feet can be
dual wielded if proficient in Unarmed Strikes.

Healing potions are deep, shimmering

are Blind while fighting with no light.

1 dungeon (or 1 level of a very large one). An oil

Necrotic Spells. Summon horrible minions or manipulate and trap targets. Some risky
necrotic spells prey upon damaged creatures, sapping their very life force—but frequently
fail to distinguish between friend and foe.
Radiant Spells. Can obliterate the unholy, the fearful, and those who would dare harm
you or your allies. Equally effective at a distance or in melee. Other spells can protect,
mend Wounds, and even restore creatures back to life!
Wind Spells. Aid in moving friend and foe alike around the battlefield. They can also
slice and cut through entire groups of foes and viciously deal extra damage on critical hits.

MAGIC

There are 6 core schools of magic. Each school has its own basic spells called cantrips and 9 Tiers
of progressively more powerful spells that can be unlocked as heroes level up. Heroes can cast any
spell from the schools that they know within the Tiers they have unlocked. Spells deal the damage
type that matches the school (unless otherwise stated).
Mana. Powerful Tiered spells require mana—a portion of the spellcaster’s life force—to fuel them.
A spell’s mana cost is equal to its Tier. Heroes gain mana as they level up. Cantrips cost no mana.

Secret Spells. Some spells (or even entire spell schools) have been hidden or lost through the
ages. Whether they are too dangerous or for other reasons, adventurers can sometimes stumble
upon new spells (or wrench them out of the undead hands of a defeated Lich).

Upcasting. Each additional mana you spend on a Tiered spell makes it stronger (up
to the Tier that you have unlocked). For example, a character that has Tier 3 spells
unlocked can upcast a Tier 1 or 2 spell at Tier 3 by spending 3 mana.

Range and Reach. As with weapons, spells with “Range” can target from a distance, but have
disadvantage if ANY enemy is adjacent to you. Add 1 die of disadvantage to gain +2 Range (max
+6). Spells with “Reach” can’t do this, but work normally in melee.

Optional Variant Rules

If your playgroup likes extra little tactical nuggets, your GM can allow you to try out some of these optional variants to add some more
spice to your game. You can always try out a rule for 1 session before committing to it.

Alternate Movement Options

Abstracted Distances. Ignore the grid (occasionally, or all the
time if you prefer) using 3 range bands instead (as always, the GM
will judge unclear situations and which creatures are affected by
abilities with an area of effect):

• Close. Creatures are effectively considered adjacent or
close enough to be affected by melee attacks or abilities
up to Reach/Range 3 without moving.
• Midrange. Creatures can be affected by thrown weapons
and shorter-range abilities (Reach/Range 4–6). Or you can
move Close by moving once.
• Far. Creatures can be affected by longer-range spells, bows,
etc. You can move Close by moving twice.

Gridless. You can forgo counting spaces and measure typical
movement from pinkie to thumb, the length of a pen or pencil,
or just eyeball it—close enough is good enough for some tables!

Diagonals NOT Adjacent. This makes for more tactical combat
as obstacles, allies, & enemies can strategically block movement,
making positioning matter much more.
Can’t Cut Corners. Similar to Diagonals NOT Adjacent, but you
cannot move diagonally past walls or enemies. A good middle
ground between full diagonal and non-diagonal movement.
1-2-1. Diagonal moves alternate in cost: the first one costs 1 Speed,
the 2nd diagonal move in a turn costs 2, and so on.

Multiclassing. If your GM wants to allow for additional creativity
for experienced players (and potentially broken combos!), when
heroes level up, they may choose ANY class. For example, when a
level 4 Berserker levels up, he could pick Commander, and take
the level 1 Commander features instead of the level 5 Berserker
features. He would have 4 d12 Hit Dice and a single d10 Hit Die.
A hero gains the equipment proficiencies of all their classes but
should use the advantaged/disadvantaged saves of whichever
class has the highest level.
“Broken” Can Be Fun! Planning, building, optimizing,
and “breaking” a class build IS the fun for many people. The
GM may need to make the game substantially more challenging if multiclassing is allowed. The GM also may veto
any particularly overpowered, unfun, or implausible combo
for the sake of the story and overall fun of the table. If this
happens, well done! Your hero was too powerful for reality.
Different Key Stats. Players can swap KEY or Secondary stats for
a class if it makes sense (e.g., DEX and WIL for the Cheat).

Custom Weapon Dice. For larger weapon die sizes, you can try
using dice of different sizes as long as they add up to the same
initial die size. For example: a 1d10 glaive could be 1d4+1d6 or
1d6+1d4 (using the first die as the Primary Die).

Level 0 Funnels. For an old school “funnel” adventure each
player gets 4 commoners to play as. Whoever survives long
enough gets to become a real level 1 adventurer! Treat each as a
d6 minion: no HP, no Wounds, no stats, no skills, or special abilities. On a player’s turn they can move and attack with all of their
commoners, and each player can Defend for 1d6 damage with a
single commoner each round. If a player loses all of their commoners, you might allow them to play as a ghost which can do 1 creative
thing per round (e.g., Frighten, distract, trip, or possess an enemy).

Healing & Resting

Fast Resting. For a much more heroic and fast-paced story, 1
Safe Rest can heal all Wounds.
Mighty Healing. Regain 1 action when healed from Dying.

Critical Healing. Treat healing just like an attack roll. Rolling
the max is a crit (roll again and add it to the total), rolling 1 is a
failure to heal (note: this variant is FUN for the right group that
enjoys big, dramatic, swingy moments). Consider incrementing
the die size by one step to compensate if you use this variant (d4
» d6 » d8 » d10 » d12).

Character Customization

Simpler Characters. New players can be overwhelmed by the
number of options and abilities in TTRPGs—consider skipping background and even ancestry (or make it cosmetic only). Any who pick
a spellcasting class like Mage, give them a smaller subset of spells
to work with initially.
Complex Characters. Heroes can pick 2 backgrounds and/or
ancestry bonuses.

Thrown Potions. Treat potions like Ranged attacks (Range 8).
The potion misses on a 1; otherwise it heals for half as much since
some splashes away and is wasted.

I HAD the High Ground. Taking a crit while flying or at a height
may cause a character to fall. A reasonable STR save may be called
for, but a weak character (e.g., a kobold) may just fall automatically.
Sucker Punch. A Prone character standing up gives adjacent
creatures the chance to take Opportunity Attacks (heroes and
monsters alike).

Playing Dead. Whenever a hero drops to 0 HP, they can attempt
to play dead by falling Prone and making an Influence check (or
other skill check as the situation demands).
Blaze of Glory. When a hero suffers their final Wound, they get
1 full turn to act before they finally die.
Retreat. The world is dangerous, and some fights may be unwinnable. Any hero may call for a retreat on their turn. If the party
agrees—unless there is a good reason story-wise that a party can’t

escape (e.g., they are trapped in nets and completely surrounded
by a band of nasty kobolds)—the GM allows them to flee. Each hero
describes their escape (e.g., casting a spell, using equipment, or
making a skill check). Consequences may follow, such as taking
damage, suffering a Wound, or failing a quest. If the escape is
particularly clever, the GM may allow the party to escape without
additional consequence. After all, the shame of retreating is often
punishment enough!
Inspiration. Whenever a party or hero does something memorable (role-plays a great moment, makes everyone laugh, misses
an attack multiple times in a row, or otherwise engages in positive
behavior), the GM can grant Inspiration: the ability to reroll any
single die. Inspiration expires after a Safe Rest.
Dramatic Leveling. Leveling up mid-combat or in another dramatic scene occasionally can make for a very memorable moment!
Just make sure everyone already knows their level up choices
beforehand to avoid slowing the game down.

FAQ

• Can I Defend/Interpose/Help after seeing the roll? Yes.
• How can I end Frightened? By spending an action to make a WIL
save, or (GM permitting) any other logical means (e.g., landing a
crit, seeing your source of fear miss, an ally’s Help).
• Defensive/Reaction abilities. Reactions resolve before their
trigger. So if an attack was made but the attacker is now Poisoned/
Frightened/Blind/etc. the attack is rerolled with that condition’s
disadvantage. You can only cast 1 defensive spell each time you
Defend (e.g., Frost Shield OR Alacrity, not both on the same round).
• KEY. Whenever a spell (or ability) uses KEY, it refers to the caster’s
attribute, not the target’s.
• Create an Opening. Flat damage abilities (e.g., Condemn), or
d44, d66, & d88 spells—these have no Primary Die, so Create an
Opening affects the next attack that does. Likewise, if the next attack
naturally crits, you may apply the +1 to the next attack.
• Interposing & AoEs? If one can be pushed out of the affected
area, AoE attacks can be Interposed.
• How does Interposing work with conditional attacks (e.g.,
an attack that can only target a Prone creature)? If it makes sense

that the hero could push their ally out of the way of the attack (e.g.,
the ally isn’t Grappled), it works as normal—the Interposing hero
suffers the damage and other effects.
• Is Grappling an attack? Yes. Anything that negatively impacts
your enemies is an attack and impacted by Rushed Attacks (you
would make the skill check with disadvantage).
• Action Cap. Spells/Abilities like Heart’s Fire and Electrocharge can
bring you above your normal action cap.
• Warding Bond. Yes, this counts as being attacked so you can
Defend against it as normal.
• Entice. Allies, multiplication effects, AoEs? Yes, you can
entice an ally. Multiplicative effects (like Rebuke’s 2× damage) multiply this damage too; AoEs likewise carry the additional damage.
• Can I Deflect damage from a crit with my shield apart from
the Defend reaction? Yes.
• Can a hidden hero Interpose? Yes, and the attack is rolled with
disadvantage as normal.
• Can Falling/Knockback damage be Defended as normal?
Yes.

Burrowing. When a creature can move normally

Cantrip. A basic spell. Costs 0 mana to cast, and

Charmed. Sees the charmer as an ally. Charmer
has advantage on social interactions with you.
Climbing. A creature with a climbing Speed
can move across vertical surfaces as flat ground;
otherwise climbing is treated as Difficult Terrain.
Concentration. Can only Concentrate on 1 thing
at a time. DC 10 STR save when crit or it breaks (see
Concentration on pg. 8 for more).

Cover. If mostly obscured, incoming attacks
have disadvantage. If completely obscured (Full
Cover), cannot typically be targeted.
Crit. Whenever a creature rolls the max on a Primary Die, that die is rolled again and added to

Wound. A measure of how close a hero is to
