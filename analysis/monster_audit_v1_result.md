# B. Findings

## 1. Rules accuracy

**R1. Reading an entry, Word version, blocker.** The Word book says, "Medium (M): dice only, no modifiers. Heavy (H): half the dice, rounded up, no modifiers" (rendered page 2), while section 3 says Medium monsters "take -10 damage from each attack" and Heavy monsters "take -20 damage from each attack." These procedures produce different fights; the embedded Markdown already has the latter procedure, so an audit of that text alone would miss the published error.

Suggested fix: replace the Word armor paragraph with the supplied rule, including the critical-hit exception.

**R2. Design standard and eight converted actions, blocker.** The standard instructs "1 Action, 2 Actions, Free, Reaction," but the controlling rule says "Monsters do NOT have 3 actions like heroes" and "only move, use the actions listed on their stat block, and their turn ends." "Gravity well," "Bore," "Bramble ring," "Bring down the slope," "Fire breath," "Whirlpool," "Lightning song," and "Smoke of forgetting" therefore have a price the monster system cannot pay, and other blocks rarely say which listed attacks are alternatives.

Suggested fix: define each monster's turn as a move and an explicit attack sequence, with named replacements and bounded triggered abilities; a custom triggered ability need not be deleted merely because its label currently says Reaction.

**R3. Saves guide and computed sheet, major.** "Heroes save against the DC printed in the action" omits the intended fallback to the single-entry stat-row DC, and the computed sheet consequently reports many genuine saves as "none." Conversely, the Iron Cobra's listed "14" comes from "Lore DC 14," while its STR save uses the header's DC 12; Examination and Influence checks on the Doppelganger and Steel Hands are also not saves.

Suggested fix: state the header fallback and distinguish saves, escape procedures and skill checks in the comparison sheet.

The guide's plain-d20 summary also omits the supplied exception that a hero-forced enemy save gains increasing advantage. It should preserve that exception rather than imply all monster saves always use one die.

**R4. Voidwracked Mage, major.** "Dying, it implodes" uses a hero state that monsters do not enter: the reference says "Monsters die when they reach 0 HP." The implosion also lacks a radius and pull distance, so repairing the trigger alone does not finish the ability.

Suggested fix: trigger it on reduction to 0 HP and specify its area and pull distance.

**R5. Flameskull, Eye of Fear and Flame, Inferno Spider Moltling and Iron Cobra, unresolved rules terms.** The attacks use "Smoldering," and the Cobra is "Immune to ... Charmed," but neither term is defined in the supplied reference or elsewhere in this book. This establishes a missing dependency in this packet, not proof that no version of Nimble has ever used either term; "Blinded" in Flash powder should likewise be normalized to the reference's "Blind."

Suggested fix: add or cite the missing definitions and use the reference's condition names consistently.

**R6. Hedge Casters and Arch Guardians, major.** Overreach "loses its next Free action" and Stitched says "Fire makes it lose its next Free action," but neither the Kartan Hedge-Mage nor Flesh Guardian has a Free ability to lose. Since monsters have no generic action pool, both advertised costs are normally empty.

Suggested fix: tie each penalty to a named action the creature actually has.

**R7. Cavern Choker, Doppelganger Sneak, Phase Spider, Slaad Tadpole and Skeletal Tomb Guardian, major.** Body shield says "Reaction, recharges on a hit," Swap never expressly identifies the triggering attack, and Ethereal repulsion, Chaos shift and Bar the way omit a per-round limit. The hero rule "Reactions cost 1 action ... each 1/round" cannot silently supply a monster action budget; in particular, repeated Chaos shifts can invalidate every ordinary melee approach while granting unlimited off-turn movement.

Suggested fix: give this class of monster ability an explicit trigger, legal targets and use limit; resolve whether the incoming attack follows movement or is cancelled.

**R8. Carrion Crawler, blocker.** Lash leaves a hero "Slowed, and held," and Bite "Crits a target that failed to break free this turn," but held is not defined, no escape procedure is supplied, and the relevant turn is unspecified. The benchmark creature therefore needs the GM to invent the very escape interaction its geometry is built around.

Suggested fix: name the actual hold condition and its escape procedure, then tie the bite rider to a specified failed escape window.

**R9. Hedge Casters, Writ-Binder, major.** Writ of seizure says "Restrained by glowing script. An ally's touch breaks it," while Restrained means "Can't move. Attacks against you have advantage." There is no duration or stated self-release, so a solo hero can remain bound even after defeating the clerk unless the GM supplies an ending.

Suggested fix: give the writ a solo-accessible escape and an explicit expiry.

**R10. Children of Ibraxus, major.** Carrier says "A Poisoned creature starting a turn beside a Child takes 1d4," and Share the fever "takes half the damage dealt to an ally within 6." With multiple Acolytes, the text does not say whether Carrier is one faction-wide effect or one trigger per Acolyte, and Share the fever does not expressly reduce the original recipient's damage or establish when the split occurs.

Suggested fix: state Carrier's source and stacking limit, and make damage sharing a defined transfer rather than possible duplicate damage.

**R11. Deathreach Ochre Jelly, major.** "Becomes two, each with half its HP. Left alone, they rejoin" does not identify current versus maximum HP, the halves' action lists, or how rejoining handles HP and the spent split. The core line and true rumour establish that splitting happens once, so an infinite-splitting accusation would overstate the evidence; the unresolved danger is two complete attack routines at one budgeted level.

Suggested fix: specify the shared once-only state, each half's current and maximum HP, and the actions and HP used when they rejoin.

**R12. Iron Cobra, major.** Bite imposes "Poisoned, Slowed or Dazed," without saying who chooses, whether the doses differ, or how long Poisoned lasts; "Make it waste three bites" also omits the important exception "unlimited in its room." The ward-word's action cost and repeatability are unspecified, potentially making one successful reading a permanent one-sided shutdown.

Suggested fix: identify dose selection and duration, and specify how speaking the word works and when it can affect the Cobra again.

**R13. Inferno Spider Moltling, major.** Molt leaves a shell that "burns anyone ending a turn beside it," without damage or duration, so full output cannot be computed. "Cool it or cut it" in the designer note also exceeds Molten web's stated exits, which are escape DC 12 or water, rather than a stated slashing release.

Suggested fix: give the shell an amount and lifetime, and make the web's escape options agree with the note.

**R14. Red Hag, major.** Bramble ring deals "1d6+2 per space moved" "Within 3 of her," but has no duration, anchoring rule or statement about forced movement. These omissions determine whether Blood calls is a positioning tool or a repeatable damage multiplier that travels with her.

Suggested fix: specify the ring's location, duration and treatment of forced movement.

**R15. Young Blizzard Dragon, blocker.** Rising winds says "aura grows to 3, then 5. Next turn," but the note promises "visible for two turns," and becoming Bloodied makes its storm "ready again" without a printed spent state or recharge rule. The GM must decide the warning period, detonation, area, repetition, continuing conditions and whether Bite and Claws accompany it.

Suggested fix: use an explicit turn-by-turn storm sequence with its reset and expiry, and state what normal attacks it replaces.

**R16. Drakkoth Faterager, major.** Battleaxe's Bloodied rider bundles "+4 damage, heals 5 on a hit once a round, and may cleave every adjacent enemy," but gives no cleave roll or frequency. The tactics say it "fights only" its named foe, which conflicts with an all-adjacent cleave unless that is a deliberate change of behaviour.

Suggested fix: define the cleave procedure and its limit, and decide whether becoming Bloodied ends its exclusive duel behaviour.

**R17. The Steel Hands, major.** "Asher Interposes for her whenever she can" invokes the hero reaction without a monster-specific eligibility or frequency rule. Interposition can be a custom monster feature, but its cost and availability must be written rather than borrowed from a nonexistent three-action reserve.

Suggested fix: give Asher's interception an explicit monster procedure and use limit.

**R18. Cultists, major presentation gap in a retained Core block.** Ecstatic outburst is labelled "Passive" with "3d6 psychic damage to all Despairing enemies," but neither the block nor index says when it happens. This audit does not invent a once-per-turn or once-per-application trigger for a printed ability whose timing is absent from the packet.

Suggested fix: add a presentation gloss quoting the intended printed timing without altering the retained damage expression.

## 2. The design standard

**S1. Arch Guardians, major.** "All members" receive Keyed and Bound in addition to their personal lists: Skeletal, Clay and Stone Guardians each have five abilities in use, and Flesh has four. The shared heading reduces duplicated text but does not meet the "at most 3 abilities (per member for families)" limit.

**S2. Flameskull, Drakkoth Faterager and Young Blizzard Dragon, major.** Fire ray packs two rays, Smoldering, a once-per-encounter area replacement and a save into one bullet; Battleaxe packs a weapon, damage change, healing and cleave; Rising winds packs an entire multi-turn state machine. Three bullets are not three simple abilities when one bullet contains several independent procedures.

**S3. Converted and original entries, mostly compliant counts.** All 44 core lines fit 22 whitespace-delimited words, every In play line fits 25, designer notes fit 45, and all required table rows fit 22; Rising winds is 21 words against the 20-word effect limit. The extra word is minor: the consequential loss is timing, geometry or escape information omitted to keep the bullet short.

**S4. Cave Fisher Angler, Dream Squire, Hitched Scarecrow, Snow Golem and Coldlight Walker, differing counterplay gaps.** "It holds one line at a time," "Wake the dreamer," "Fire does double damage," "The child who made it can unmake it," and "Cover the lantern and it goes as blind as you" live in rumours or designer prose. This does not make them false: the Fisher's cap and Scarecrow's vulnerability are complete facts needing better placement, Snow Golem's creator is a valid narrative route, while the Squire needs an intervention procedure and the Walker's rumour exceeds the written lantern effect.

Suggested fix: use one authoritative place for mechanically consequential vulnerabilities, with the rumour explaining how a hero discovers them.

**S5. Far Wanderer, minor canon consideration.** "It can't stretch what it can't see" supplies a useful sight restriction, while the encounter "Each knot opens a tunnel" establishes a larger narrative power than its combat actions. These facts can stand as written, but a player who steals a cord has not thereby been promised the Wanderer's ability to open tunnels; that transfer would be a separate ruling.

**S6. Grell and Dark Creeper, uncertainty about senses.** "It hunts by sound" gives the Grell a useful sensory clue, while the Creeper's false rumour combines "They see in the dark like cats" with "fighting without light is hopeless." A compound false rumour does not establish that every clause is false, so it cannot settle the Creeper's vision; its own ability to see in Snuff's darkness remains unstated and materially affects its attack advantage.

**S7. Rank-and-File Dead, Spiders and Briarbanes, usable limits.** "On death, reset to 1 HP," "Restrained, escape DC 12 or any slashing or fire damage," and "Being hit degrades armor" identify a trigger and a visible change directly. These entries show why the short format works when the counterplay remains in the action text; no new rewrite is required for them.

**S8. Eye of Fear and Flame and Hitched Scarecrow, minor combination requirement.** Their "Already Frightened, Dazed" rider does not normally progress from their own previous gaze: Frightened expires at the end of the hero's intervening turn. Another fear source or an expressly allowed second gaze is needed, so these riders must not be counted as automatic Dazed every subsequent round.

## 3. Play in this campaign

**P1. The Gilded Hands, blocker.** Contract says every member "disengages for free and leaves," reinforced by "there is never a clean kill to end the thread." If this overrides manacles, blocked exits or a hero holding the prize, a Steal or Stop episode ends by author guarantee rather than by the heroes' choices; a rival crew can be recurring without guaranteed survival.

Suggested fix: make retreat an attempted movement through an available route, with capture, defeat and negotiated surrender able to close its thread.

**P2. The Gilded Hands and The Steel Hands, major solo preparation gap.** The Gilded Hands total 3+2+2+2=9 monster levels and four non-minions; one each of Kalya, Hand, Crossbow and Asher totals 1+3+2+5=11. Those are not automatically solo encounters merely because they are possible NPC-room results, and "for higher parties, add a level each" changes the budget without specifying HP, damage, DC or which party sizes it covers.

**P3. Neogi Hatchling Swarm, major.** Mind leash requires "adult neogi within 12," its tactics scatter when that adult falls, and the encounter adds "an adult neogi" on a "captured hulk," but no adult profile or proxy is supplied. The stated counterplay, encounter budget and even the swarm's principal controller feature consequently depend on a second book or an improvised monster.

Suggested fix: provide a budgeted adult proxy and state what happens to an existing leash when it falls.

**P4. Phase Spider, major.** Phase makes it vanish until "the start of its next turn," and its tactics are "bites, blinks away." Ordinary hero turns therefore offer no target, and the packet supplies no general interrupt or prepared-attack procedure that makes the promised hunt reliably playable for a solo melee hero.

Suggested fix: give the heroes a defined attack window or a stated, achievable way to force its return.

**P5. Slaad Tadpole, major.** "Chaos shift (Reaction, attacked in melee)" has no limit, while Grow leads to an adult slaad and the patron hook asks for "The cure ... in Terrache" without a profile, cure or lead. Even after its evasion is bounded, its main clock cannot be resolved from this entry alone, and "a good meal" is an undefined way to accelerate it.

Suggested fix: define one concrete growth trigger and a playable cure lead or adult proxy for the clock's completion.

**P6. Hell Hound, major episode risk.** "It can track anyone who has been to Maphotar, anywhere" has no scent requirement, acquisition procedure, range, uncertainty or way to break the trail. If read as perfect target detection rather than following an acquired trail, this can remove hiding or answer a Find episode before play starts; it does not itself establish instant knowledge of whereabouts or passage through controlled arches, so those powers must not be assumed.

Suggested fix: establish how it acquires a particular trail, how travel through arches affects pursuit, and a discoverable way to interrupt it.

**P7. Dream Squire and Meenlock Stalker, major.** "Everyone it passes falls asleep standing" must not incapacitate heroes under the conversion agency rule, while "dropped, it wakes at dawn unless its dream is broken" and "Victims turn unless the meenlock that marked them burns" introduce consequential return or transformation rules without complete procedures. These are not errors merely because they affect the fiction; the problem is that the intervention and deadline needed to finish the gig are unclear.

Suggested fix for Dream Squire: define awakening its dreamer and make the passing sleep an NPC description or the printed Slowed/Dazed effect. Suggested fix for Meenlock: state the mark, transformation deadline and cure as an explicit, playable consequence.

**P8. Gibbering Mouther, Yeti Howler and Swarm of Flying Snakes, major.** "The GM picks where they move," "carried off at full Speed," and "carrying a Small creature" preserve attacks in principle, but leave movement expenditure or escape unresolved. The agency standard expressly permits forced movement, so the criticism is missing procedure, particularly when a lone hero has no ally to dig them out or recover them.

Suggested fix: state whether movement is forced or spends an action, then give carried or buried heroes a self-rescue procedure.

**P9. Grimlocks of Teveron, major.** "Start the Waking clock (4)" and "Advance ... one step each round" establish a clear trigger and pace, but never establish what completion does or how the party can intervene. Killing the chanter is a player choice, so the subsequent automatic round ticks do not violate the faction-clock guardrail; the gap is the unprepared climax four rounds later.

Suggested fix: give completion one concrete effect and one visible intervention or escape condition, allowing the larger threat to become a thread.

**P10. Water Myrmidon, Slaad Tadpole and Snow Golem, minor indexing errors.** Their stat rows say "Stage 13+," but their own Gig Fit or encounters place them in "Stages 9-10," "Stages 9-10," and "the City of Arches in winter" respectively. These are allowed thematic placements, not wrong levels; they need to be discoverable from the same Stage selector used at the table.

**P11. Arch Guardians, major consistency gap.** "Keyed" ignores only the person carrying a key, whereas patrons routinely send a party through a shared route, and the encounter deliberately has the guardian object to a companion without one. The campaign needs a consistent rule for whether a key covers companions, can be passed along, or merely makes its bearer untouchable; "the opening fight of an Arch direction" should also remain an eligibility instruction when a permitted room or But+ result calls for it, not a compulsory faction introduction.

Suggested fix: state the scope of a key's protection and retain "Any arch" as eligibility, rather than changing the deliberately broad Stage.

**P12. Patron hooks, mostly fitting, with specific gaps.** "The Archkeepers want an object back" in Carrion Crawler does not explain whose safety or route depends on it, while the University wanting notes, specimens or the guardian's key-detection method directly follows its method. Knights taking bribes, covert patronage from Joslyn, and Council or University buyers can be campaign additions, but need to stay subordinate to the patrons' stated wants rather than turn every institution into an interchangeable secret purchaser.

## 4. Power and balance

The calculations below compare every non-minion member with its Builder level and armor. They do not treat retained Core numbers, the two Ochre Jellies, Clay Guardian fire healing or the Basilisk's Lithify as mistakes.

**Method.** `E` is expected attack HP damage before armor, Defense or other mitigation, including an initial Primary Die 1 missing and an exploding maximum. For a primary dN plus secondary-die mean and flat bonus `B`, `E = N(N+1)/(2(N-1)) - 1/N + ((N-1)/N)B`; secondary dice are not independently exploded. For a no-crit attack, the primary contribution is `(N+1)/2 - 1/N`. An exclamation mark after an expression below means **cannot crit**, not an extra die; Bandit also misses on 8.

`S` denotes a save-gated area effect, shown as its raw mean on a failed save and the stated half where applicable; its attack/miss/crit interaction is not established by this packet. `F` denotes automatic damage shown as a raw mean, not a claim that "can't miss" also means "can't crit"; for such attacks it is a known component, potentially increased by critical damage. Save probabilities need the attending hero's modifier, and advantage probabilities need fuller rules than supplied, so neither is invented. Decimal-dice resolution, particularly how a Primary Die 1 is read in d44/d88, is also not settled here.

The Builder's 2- and 3-attack columns are **per attack**. Thus a level-3 two-attack routine is compared with `2×6=12`, not 14 per attack or 28 per round. Its sample dice and targets are approximate and sometimes differ from exact expected damage under the supplied miss/crit rules; a small discrepancy is not a mechanical fault.

Where turn sequencing is undefined, the table states the conditional extra output rather than pretending to know which actions replace others. Recurring triggers, summons and Digested have no single fixed maximum DPR: their required parameters are shown. The **power** judgement includes durability and usable control, not just the basic damage comparison.

Regeneration estimates assume the monster's own turn: "Heals 5 each turn" on Flameskull and "Heals 5 a turn" on Snow Golem should say so if that is intended, since healing on every combatant's turn scales sharply with attendance.

**Minion and summon arithmetic.** Kobold d4 minions average `(2+3+4)/4=2.25`; Goblin, Ooze, Dig-Hand, snake and scarab d6 minions average `20/6=3.33`; Watch Guards and spider d8 minions average `35/8=4.375`. Bandit minions also have Inept, so their d8 averages `(2+3+4+5+6+7)/8=3.375`; none of these explode, use advantage, or have HP, and attacks by minions on the same hero are combined before Defense/Interpose. The 1 HP mirror-mask decoys are not labelled minions and do not attack, so they are not a minion-rule violation.

**Ooze full-output qualification.** Against one initially unmarked hero, Gray Ooze is `4.03+1.67t`, Core Ochre Jelly `9.03+2.50t`, Black Pudding `21.54+8.33t`, and Elder Ooze `39.60+15t`, with `t` the target's Digested count at the start of the routine and every attack kept on that hero. The latter two include expected within-routine stacking, assuming Digested increments on a successful damaging touch; Goopy then adds surviving minion waves, so merely quoting 18.1 or 27.1 as their full output would understate them.

**HP discrepancies that matter in the new entries.** Maren is `41/27=1.52` of her Medium benchmark, Writ-Binder `38/27=1.41`, Duergar Delver and Lizardfolk Warden each `31/19=1.63`, Steel Hand Crossbow `24/19=1.26`, and Asher `64/49=1.31`. These Standard profiles have no stated Elite explanation; high armor is a specialization rather than a free addition to the unarmored HP column.

**Elite exceptions that are intentional.** Grell's 80 HP is below the level-7 unarmored 87 HP reference, Deathreach Jelly's 62 is near level-5 64, and Blizzard Dragon's 95 Medium HP exactly matches level 9. Those meet the permitted "roughly two levels up" approach; their concerns are attack output, splitting, timing and encounter budgeting, not an assertion that Elite HP must equal Standard HP.

**Clear damage departures in original families.** Blade-Dancer's two d6+3 attacks yield `13.07/8=1.63` of the level-2 two-attack target; Maren yields `15.29/12=1.27`, Tobin `11.40/8=1.43`, and a Golden Knight `14.73/12=1.23` before their extra benefits. In the other direction, Hedge-Mage's `11.12/17=0.65` is not high direct damage for its level, Voice of the Idol's `13.62/20=0.68` is low before control, and Eye's `14.21/24=0.59` remains low unless the undefined Smoldering adds substantial damage.

**Armor introduction is a party-level issue.** The reference says "Medium Armor monsters should not be introduced any earlier than level 3" and "Save Heavy Armor until the heroes are level 8+." A level-2 Medium profile is not automatically illegal when encountered by higher-level heroes, but a Stage-based lookup does not enforce that party-level gate; Golden Knight pairs add an especially consequential early Heavy Armor formation.

## 5. Generated-content patterns and canon

**G1. True-rumour counterplay has drifted across families, major.** Snakemen say "Fire or a blade frees you ... a crit on either of you breaks it," combining releases printed for Briarbane Tangle and Mimic Sticky; Mimics claim they "cannot move once ... stuck" despite Speed 6 and no stated immobility. These are unsupported within this packet; the full Grapple rule was not supplied, so this finding does not claim to have disproved every possible general escape rule.

**G2. Several other true rumours teach an absent immunity, major.** A missed Specter is supposedly "gone for a round," a Cloaker supposedly changes its half-damage transfer if you "Attack it from range," and a stationary hero supposedly makes the Worm "lose you." The blocks give no missed-touch disappearance, halve attacks regardless of range while Grappling, and merely give the Worm disadvantage against an unmoving target.

**G3. Core tactical explanations sometimes reverse the mechanic, major.** Bandits are called "fragile (a 2 or less misses them)," although missing on an extra result improves defense; the Goblin note's "block the retaliation" must not be read as Defend, since that retaliation ignores Defense. "Put the lightest in front" does not defeat the Bulette's initial heaviest-target selection, "get under" the Giant's Reach 2 does not establish a minimum range, and removing metal plate does not make a hero immune to drake lightning.

**G4. Counterplay is sometimes an asserted consequence rather than an executable rule, major.** Stirges supposedly flee "A bright lantern" while In play says they break off only when dead; Coldlight Walker's lantern-covering only switches off Coldlight, not a printed sense or Blind condition; Eye's "no rays until its next turn" does not sacrifice the normal rays on that next turn. Meenlock's bright-light limit is on Shadow step, not Horrid link, so torches do not end its damage link.

Suggested fix for this pattern: make every mechanically tactical true rumour point to an actual action, trait or expressly stated behaviour, and remove unsupported promises of certainty.

**G5. Recurring templates narrow the bestiary's actual play, major design pattern.** "Already [condition], Dazed" recurs on Dream Squire, Eye and Scarecrow, with related ratchets on Meenlock and the Children; "Bloodied ... leaves" or its equivalent recurs on Doppelganger, Choker, Wanderer, Fisher, Grell, Lizardfolk, Creeper, Hag, Spider, Ghast, Dragon and Alchemist. Rescue victims, a goal behind a body, specimens for scholars and covert Black Hand shipments recur so often that different worlds can produce the same session even where the core line changes.

Suggested fix for this pattern: vary a few creatures' demands, responses to injury and ways of completing a gig; do not add a new mechanic to every entry.

**G6. Important setting additions are labelled or implied as established canon, major provenance issue.** "Vynan origin is canon" is supported for replacement agents, but "Gorvund the djinni," "the Yondoth's ghost-engine," "Celevis's ... sentinels ... [asleep] a thousand years," "Threska's council wizards," and "Klamas's fortress" go beyond section 4. These may be appropriate new canon, but their species identities, histories and causal explanations were not verified by the supplied list.

**G7. Faction and institution additions need a deliberate decision, not an invented correction.** The book asserts the Fetid Prince title and plague theology, Vithra's blood-idol rites, a Knights/Black Hand truce, a statue of Lord Salvana, keys as Erna's payment, the bear-lodge prohibition on killing yetis, and a Choul gateway; section 4 does not establish those claims. "Commander Joslyn" need not contradict Bianca's command if it is a subordinate title, but the wording must not silently replace the stated chain of command; "Verosa" likewise has no grounding in the supplied canon list.

**G8. Naming is a practical collision, minor.** "The Gilded Hands," "The Steel Hands," "the Black Hand," and local slang "The Hands" put three recurring organizations on near-identical cues. No provided canon name proves the Gilded Hands impossible; the risk is a returning player confusing which debt, crew or employer the recap means.

Across all 63 headers there are **29 Controllers, 10 Lurkers, 10 Skirmishers, 8 Brutes, 5 Defenders and 1 Artillery**. Lost Cisterns and Sunken Revvia share many broad-range Core families rather than receiving equally specific new ecology. The danger is not having too few stat blocks, but lacking a fast filter that combines site, budget, party size and usable counterplay.

There is no net-new Boss header among the converted/original entries; their three Elite entries are Grell, Deathreach Jelly and Blizzard Dragon. A room labelled boss can use an ordinary creature and a meaningful objective, so this is not proof of missing content; it does mean the book should not call a Standard creature "solo-grade" and silently grant it legendary survivability or action economy against an entire party.

## SEB and table use

SEB has kept the individual active blocks short and the new core lines specific: the knotting surveyor, word-stopped Cobra and sleeper-linked Feyr have usable central interactions. The main loss is procedure: escape conditions, expiry, a defined event window and whether an action replaces an attack are often pushed out of the box or omitted, while several bullets retain multiple independent riders.

The Word book renders to 159 pages. The inspected roster/active-block pages were legible, but the contents list has no page references and the file has no Word bookmarks or hyperlinks; the recurring encounter, lair and rumour tables therefore still require manual navigation. This was a content audit with a visual spot-check, not certification of every page's layout.

The required four table sets are present for every entry. Converted/original table rows meet the word cap, but some lairs mostly describe a place without an identifiable past event or behaviour clue: the Children's "drain-mouth chapel ... every wall damp" is atmosphere, whereas a dead guardpost "guards sorted into neat piles" tells the GM what the crawler did. There is no need to add the deliberately deferred hoard, speech, number-appearing fields or Elite lairs during this correction pass.

## Ten fixes first

1. **Correct the Word armor guide and align the two source versions.** Every armored fight depends on this, and the embedded prompt currently conceals the publication error.
2. **Establish monster turn grammar.** Resolve the eight 2-Action abilities, attack replacements, header DC fallback and custom off-turn limits before trying to tune DPR.
3. **Finish the minimum procedures needed to run a block.** Prioritize Crawler holds, Writ self-release, Choker redirection, Jelly splitting, Molt shell damage, Cobra doses and Voidwracked Mage's death event.
4. **Remove episode-level guarantees.** Make Contract contestable and bound Hell Hound tracking so achieved objectives, capture and concealment remain possible.
5. **Make the true rumours reliable.** Correct transferred escapes, nonexistent disappearances, range-based immunity claims and promised shutdowns that their blocks do not implement.
6. **Repair the Blizzard storm and Faterager's Bloodied routine.** Both need explicit timing or target procedure before their dramatic set pieces can be budgeted or telegraphed honestly.
7. **Reconcile Standard HP and damage departures.** Start with Maren, Writ-Binder, Delver, Warden, Crossbow Hand, Asher and Blade-Dancer; retain excess power only as a deliberate, visible budget decision.
8. **Make the solo filter concrete.** Specify which crew members appear in a solo gig, ensure a lone hero can escape each hold, and give Phase Spider a response window and the tadpole/adult-neogi clocks usable endpoints.
9. **Register consequential new canon and make hooks match it.** Decide Gorvund, Klamas, the Yondoth engine, Vithra, Choul, lodge law and faction arrangements instead of treating source labels as verification.
10. **Add one navigation and consistency pass.** Reconcile Stage exceptions, make rival-clock advancement/resolution explicit, add a site/level/role index with page links, and regenerate the corrected numeric sheet from actual saves and attack routines.

These priorities are triage for a later edit. No monster, canon statement or GM decision has been changed by this audit, so the supplied triage template's "Fixed now" category would remain empty.
