# Sejr: campaign overview

Oct 11, 2026 · @Sebastian

## Pitch and premise

Sejr is an episodic, drop-in Nimble campaign in the City of Arches: one gig per session, played by whoever shows, with arches as cheap transport that patrons control.

The city is thousands of years old and no one controls the arches, but keys exist, and whoever holds a key decides where an arch opens. In Sejr keys are not rare for patrons, so the city's politics are route politics: who can move people and goods wins. The outer regions are neglected, a meta layer the party never sees directly and only meets through consequences.

Heroes are professionals with parallel lives and professional courtesy, thrown together on gigs. Each starts with an unexamined arcanum and a holding in the city. The game is play to find out, with a short list of patrons who return and evolve.

## Format and logistics

Sessions run weekly for three hours with unknown attendance, so every session is a bottle episode that works for a solo player or a full table.

- One gig per session, drawn on the mat, with the arch as the door in and out of the map.
- First session is planned for after Christmas 2026, with a pitch to the players beforehand.
- Players know 5e, so Nimble is taught through its 5e overlap. Characters are quick to make and easy to manage.
- Heroes who are absent are elsewhere in the city. Their players tick an arcana ritual instead of losing progress.
- Levels follow the group: new or returning players join at average group level, framed as the same hero "with a beard" in Mørk Borg style.
- Milestone levelling every two to three episodes, so attendance never splits the party.
- Renown on the episode tables is capped at average group level.

The door picks who is asking, the mission builder varies what, why and what goes wrong, and But+ interrupts each scene. When nobody picks a door, the hottest clock goes first. Every session closes by swapping one thread.

## Generators and tables

Each episode is rolled from three chained d6 tables, then interrupted scene by scene. Rolls scale past the die maximum as renown rises, so heroes grow out of the easy results.

| Table | Roll | Gives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direction | Choice, or d6 (1-3 City, 4-6 Arch) | The door for the episode |
| Stage | d6 + renown, capped at average level | 1-6 city sites, 7-8 Lower Reaches, 9-10 Lost Cisterns, 11-12 Sunken Revvia, 13+ through an arch |
| Verb | d6 | Find, escort, stop, steal, bury, prove |
| But | d6 | Wrong target, goal flips, patron is the threat, place shifts genre, arch or key intervenes, clock hits early |
| But+ | d6 + scene number | 1-2 foreshadow, 3 faction encounter, 4 monster, 5 twist, 6 dusk, 7+ dark |
| Rooms | d10 + average level / 3 | Empty, trap, hazard, solo, NPC, mob, major hazard, treasure, boss |
| Rival crew | Inside an NPC room | At most once per episode, promoted to the tracker if met twice |

City Stages 1-6 are Market Square festival, Villas of the Second Rise, Public Baths, Cliffs of the Dead, Theater of Whispers and the Arch of Kings.

The tables live in a fork of a Shadowdark dungeon and scenario generator on Perchance. The fork adds a renown and level input, an Arch or City toggle, the But and But+ tables, Arches rumors for the thread board, and a hero card for chargen. Two panels were drafted (lists and HTML) and have not been tested inside Perchance yet.

Factions and fronts enter play only through But+ and the NPC rooms, never through prep, so threats emerge from play.

## Patrons and armatures

Four patrons return and evolve, and each has a fixed armature so dice or a tired GM cannot make them act out of character. Dice roll the how, never the who or why.

| Patron | Wants | Method | Pays / takes | Secret (draft) | Stages |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Archkeepers | Nobody hurt, city undisturbed | Kindness first, then the Golden Knights; they omit, never lie | Pay: key passage, shelter. Take: goodwill | Some arrivals were sent on purpose | Welcoming, wary, gatekeeping |
| Black Hand | Profit nobody can trace | Cut-outs and deniability, violence out of sight | Pay: cash, silence. Take: leverage | Quietly acquiring scholars who can read keys | Errands, entangled, owned |
| Golden Council | Order, and credit for it | Writs, licenses, audits via Roselyn Zeshe | Pay: standing, pardons. Take: your name on the record | Prefers a problem stays useful to justify the Knights | Contracting, auditing, conscripting |
| University | Understand the method, then own it | Examination, grants, tutors | Pay: training, ritual ticks. Take: findings, published as its own | A key-making program | Open lectures, licensing, monopoly |

The University is new to the setting; Kartan is its nearest canon relative. Secrets are drafts: either fixed now, or three candidates per patron locked in on first contact.

Same verb, different success. For "find the relic" the Archkeepers want it returned unopened, the Black Hand want it sold quietly, the Council want it logged and the University want it examined.

**Guardrails**

- A twist is filtered by asking which version of it fits this patron.
- Reactions are not rolled: when the party refuses or betrays a patron, it answers with its Method and Currency.
- Stages move on table choices only. But+ can foreshadow a stage, never trigger it.
- A party-owned key is a mid-campaign prize that offends one patron and becomes a thread.

## Arches, keys and the university

Power in Sejr settles with whoever learns to read and make keys, and a university is the natural home for that knowledge. The Grainbound worksheets (metal, fuel, four dials, invention of the nation) were used as lenses.

- Metal: the keys are the ore, buried beneath the city, with the Lower Reaches (Black Hand ground) as the mine. The scholar who can read or tune a key is the smith, the one bottleneck that can walk away. The guild binds scholars with oaths, ranks and tuition; the Black Hand buys or kidnaps them.
- Key tiers from canon: one-use, daily, at-will and two-way keys. Each rung is a guild technology.
- Fuel: whatever powers a key is unknown in canon. A heat tax becomes a passage toll, with patrons exempt and the Lower Reaches unable to pay.
- Dependence starts Open Sky and slides toward One Tap as the guild learns to read or copy keys.
- Exit cost: the more arches are used, the more roads and outer lands are neglected, so those without keys face a Sealed Cage they never see close.
- Institutions: the guild sits beside the Golden Council (Community), or the Queen's spymaster and Kartan's court mages capture it (Palace).
- Magic's reach: an unexamined arcanum in every hero's hands is Commons Magic; a university that licenses examination turns it into Court Magic. Optional enrollment keeps that dial live.
- Nation: arrivals have no memory, so the city is their first loyalty. The Archkeepers' gift basket is a shared moment, the speaking relic is a language field, and the ink belongs to whoever registers arrivals.

**University structure.** A hero's career starts at their first arcanum. Ranks follow the arcana ritual: Novice (unexamined), Reader (first trait revealed), Wielder (reliable use, unlocks patron jobs), Cracker (breaks it open; final ritual step is always played at the table). Benefits are adventure (job board, first call from patrons), a trade chapter (a specialty and a holding upgrade slot) and arcana training between episodes. Enrollment is optional, and un-enrolled heroes advance arcana only through play. The guild has schisms (Traditionalists, Royalists, a New School) and a quiet enforcement wing of ex-adventurers.

**Canon flag.** The book says no one controls the arches and keys are exceedingly rare. Key-making breaks the first, so it runs as a front, not as fact: three stages, Find, Read, Make, with the table starting at Find and the guild creeping toward Read. Each stage is a grim portent for the thread board.

## Characters

Chargen is: pick a class, ancestry, skills and one arcanum (class or random), leave the arcanum unexamined and unchecked, get a holding, and go. Heroes start at level 1 with parallel lives and professional courtesy.

- Nimble stats stay. Characterization comes from TBE-style tags: one descriptor for each of the class's two KEY stats, plus two personality traits.
- Tags can be invoked once per hero per episode for advantage, at the GM's call. Taking disadvantage from a trait ticks the arcana ritual. Whether the limit is per hero or per tag is undecided; per hero is the recommendation.
- Three life events (Origin, Youth, Recent), written for the Arches in the TBE style. Each gives a tag, two questions for the player, a choice of two A5e skills and a tie (friend, patron, rival or adversary) that becomes a thread.
- A5e skill list as the table's vocabulary, with specialties from A5e. The player argues which Nimble skill applies from the sheet and the fiction, and a stretch gets disadvantage, never a flat no. Specialties give advantage: two at level 1, one more at levels 5, 9, 13 and 17, plus INT if positive.
- Look, habit, secret and a faction mark (Table 4 of the Arches homebrew) round out the hero. The holding is a city location plus a quirk.
- Shared history: when two heroes first share a gig, each can reinterpret a life event to involve the other and gain a specialty, capped at two per hero, with different partners.
- Returning heroes roll a beard result on return: a scar, a patron debt, a contact, a rumor that reveals a Neglect clock, a changed holding, or a stirred arcanum.

## Bia, the DMPC

Bia is a minotaur paladin who anchors the party when attendance is thin, and she must never become the spotlight. The campaign is about the game and the setting, not about her.

- Frenzy: when Bloodied, Bia saves. On a fail she swaps to a labyrinth-beast stat block and attacks the nearest creature. A player runs her in frenzy, never the GM.
- Turn back, pick two or three: healed above Bloodied, someone speaks her name or oath words (Influence, DC 12), three rounds pass, she harms someone she loves.
- Tally: mark paladin each time she turns back by choice and beast each time she needs rescue. When one side hits a number, the table learns who she is. At level 3 the tally can pick her subclass.
- A healing guardian companion is the turn-back anchor, so her pain is shared and the guardian going down is the real threat.
- DMPC rules: she never knows the answer, never solves the scene, and counts as a hero for encounter budgeting so a lone player still gets a fair fight.

## Trackers

Prep is the trackers and nothing else. Every episode closes one thread and opens one, and the trackers carry the memory a drop-in table lacks.

| Tracker | Holds | Moves when | Payoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Thread board | Six index cards: hero ties, arcana, Bia's labyrinth past, Arches rumors, gazetteer hooks | One closes and one opens each episode | Next episode's door |
| Neglect clocks | One hidden clock per skipped place: Marshes, Hills, Deadlands, Borderlands, Lower Reaches | Each City episode ticks the place the party skipped | A full clock reaches the holding as refugees, raiders, prices or a ruined supplier; commit to one payoff every 3-4 episodes |
| Faction clocks | One six-step clock per patron and per local band or rival crew met | Player choices, never But+ alone | A full clock becomes a thread and the faction acts in the city |
| Renown | A number, +1 per episode | Each episode finished | Capped at average group level; sets Stage and room scaling |
| Arcana ritual | One track per hero | Absent players tick once; trait disadvantage ticks; play can tick more | The final step is only played at the table |

A rival crew met twice, or a local band named on first meeting, goes onto the faction tracker. A "last time" line opens every session so absent players re-enter in one sentence.

## Decisions made

- System: Nimble, in the City of Arches, with Stonetop-style arcana and A5e skill vocabulary.
- Arches are cheap transport for patrons. Keys are not rare for patrons, and the outer regions are neglected as a hidden layer.
- The home base is the city and the party holds a holding there. Tables point two ways, Arch or City.
- Missions differ by patron: same verb, different success condition. Motives differ by patron and by hero stake.
- A short list of four patrons returns and evolves, each with a fixed armature.
- A university, entered at a hero's first arcanum, is the guild that trains wielding and cracking arcana. Enrollment is optional.
- Players join at average group level, with a "beard" return roll. Renown is capped at average level.
- Factions and threats enter only through interruption tables, and the trackers carry memory.
- Missions rely on fail forward: a failure moves a clock, never ends the session.
- The generator is a Perchance fork of a Shadowdark generator, with chargen included.
